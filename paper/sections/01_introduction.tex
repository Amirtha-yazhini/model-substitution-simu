\section{Introduction and Threat Model}\label{sec:intro}
Large Language Models are increasingly getting adapted via an intermediate like an api
provider,here a client pays for a certain model ,gets the response along with the model's name
,which by itself cannot prove for an occurence of model substitution~\cite{cai2025} by the provider
or its absence. Substitution can occur for several reasons including monetary incentives which
proves to be large.For the token mix used in this paper the substitute costs 11.5\% of the genuine
model, which means that a gateway that swaps one request in ten saves about \$88 per \$1,000 of
billed traffic.

So far trusted hardware based attestation method~\cite{cai2025} proves to be the only definite way
to check this,but gateways do not offer such methods today.What a
client can do is check the responses using black box auditors .They send probe requests and based
on the response which include text,timing and other metadata, they can check whether the
required/advertised model has actually answered.

These black-box auditors employ several statistical methods. From April 2025 onwards at least 7
such auditors have been proposed~\cite{cai2025,zhu2025rut,lin2026gatescope,fang2026kbf,bruckner2026,iris2026,difr2025}.
Each of them were tested on their own test-bed with their own adversaries.They choose thresholds
chosen on the same data which is reported. A frozen-threshold replication~\cite{holdout2026} shows
how fragile this is in practice. A method with perfect accuracy during development fell to 50\%
sensitivity on fresh model-pairs and half of those pairs could not be scored at all. The proposed
auditing methods so far have never been compared on a common testbed against a common adversary,
and the papers that consider a gateway which can detect auditing~\cite{cai2025,zhu2025rut,iris2026}
test it only against their own probes.

\textbf{Threat model.} The user sends HTTPS requests to a gateway that seems to provide model $M$.
Routing and response metadata is under complete control of the gateway.For each request three
things may happen: the gateway may forward it to $M$,to a cheaper subsititute $M'$ to another
provider of $M$ or to a cache.Apart from this, it may also rewrite the reported model name, the
\texttt{usage} toen counts and \texttt{system\_fingerprint} and some delay may also be
added.Classification of requests by their shape is also possible.In order to simulate a real-life
adversary the goal of the gateway becomes to save as much as possible while keeping the
probability of detection low.The clients get to see the response text,client-side latency, the
\texttt{usage} block, the fingerprint and the logprobs where the endpoint expose them.
Timing can be measured by the client on their own the other signal is the content.To test for
false positives honest inference variations such as load balancing across legitimate providers of
$M$ is permitted.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A shared adversarial testbed:} SHIM is an OpenAI-compatible gateway which has twelve
  adversary arms and a hidden ledger with the answers. Arms such as probe-aware
  evasion and canary-awarness were studied before for single detectors~\cite{cai2025,iris2026};
  here every arm runs against every detector
\item \textbf{A sealed comparision of six auditors:} We have reimplemented five of the published
  auditing methods and added an e-value fusion. All of them which are evaluated with thresholds
  are hashed and tagged appropriately. An auditor that cannot run on a specific arm reports as
  `uninformative'.
\item \textbf{Practical Deployability:} None of the endpoints returns the logprobs our version of
  the rank-based auditor needs. The most recent one token fingerprint method
  runs on 4 of them as hidden reasoning steps are present in many of the real life models.
\item \textbf{Three negative results:} An auditor becomes chance-like in presence of a probe-aware
  evasion.False positive budgets set a dilution floor of $\epsilon^* = 0.10$. Timing based auditors
  cannot tell fraud from sanctioned routing.
\end{itemize}
