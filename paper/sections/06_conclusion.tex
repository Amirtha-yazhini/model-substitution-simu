\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\textbf{Limitations.} (i)~\emph{The grid runs on simulated models.} Calibration, holdout, grid,
dilution sweep and confound all use SHIM's mock backend. The fitted ladder takes answer
distributions and latency from real measurements, but surface habits and accuracy are still
hand-written, and the post-hoc IRIS-lite check shows that this can move $\epsilon^*$. The coverage,
real-response and live results come from real endpoints; the grid's power numbers describe the
mock. (ii)~\emph{One real session per endpoint.} Free-tier quotas allowed one 240-request session
per endpoint, so thresholds could not be calibrated on real traffic, and real-endpoint results use
permutation p-values instead of sealed thresholds. (iii)~\emph{Reimplementations at reduced
operating points.} None of the auditors is the original authors' code. OTE uses 8 of 40 cells,
IRIS-lite 16 of 179 features, KBF and BENCH 15 and 12 items, and GATEOPS omits GateScope's memory
channel. Our results may therefore understate the original methods. (iv)~\emph{One free tier at one
point in time.} Groq served nearly all real traffic; the other providers returned payment errors or
ran out of daily quota. Free tiers change from month to month. (v)~\emph{Simulated adversaries and a
small live run.} A5 is our own classifier, and a real one could be better or worse. The live run is
96 requests per arm on one provider, and benign routing could not be run live.

\textbf{Conclusion.} We ran five published model substitution auditors and one new fusion against a
common adversarial gateway, under thresholds sealed before evaluation. An audit session costs
well under a cent, but a gateway that recognises audit-shaped
requests keeps every auditor at chance. The requests it recognises are short prompts with small
token caps, which is also what keeps probes cheap. Below $\epsilon^* \approx 0.10$--$0.15$ no admissible audit detects dilution, because the false-positive
budget caps how often an audit can be repeated. Timing auditors flag
sanctioned routing more readily than fraud. Content can reveal identity violations, but not
disclosure or billing violations. Verifiable accounting for LLM
APIs will need probes that cannot be told apart from production traffic, disclosure of routing, and
attested metering. Black-box statistics alone cannot provide the last two.

\textbf{Artifact.} The gateway, auditors, sealed protocols, replay corpus and every figure are
available in an anonymised repository:
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/model-substitution-simu-0F5B}. All results except the live
run can be regenerated without an API key.
