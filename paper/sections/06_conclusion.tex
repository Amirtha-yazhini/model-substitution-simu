\section{Limitations and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\textbf{Limitations.} Five matter most. (i)~\emph{The grid runs on simulated models.} Calibration,
holdout, grid, dilution sweep and confound all use SHIM's mock backend. The fitted ladder takes
answer distributions and latency from real measurements, but surface habits and accuracy are still
hand-written, and the post-hoc IRIS-lite check shows that this can move $\epsilon^*$. The coverage,
real-response and live results come from real endpoints; the grid's power numbers describe the
mock. (ii)~\emph{One real session per endpoint.} Free-tier quotas allowed one 240-request session
per endpoint, so thresholds could not be calibrated on real traffic, and real-endpoint results use
permutation p-values instead of sealed thresholds. (iii)~\emph{Reimplementations at reduced
operating points.} None of the auditors is the original authors' code. OTE uses 8 of 40 cells,
IRIS-lite 16 of 179 features, KBF and BENCH 15 and 12 items, and GATEOPS omits GateScope's memory
channel. Our results may therefore understate the original methods. (iv)~\emph{One free tier at one point
in time.} Groq served nearly all real traffic; the other providers returned payment errors or ran
out of daily quota. Free tiers change from month to month. (v)~\emph{Simulated adversaries and a
small live run.} A5 is our own classifier, and a real one could be better or worse. The live run is
96 requests per arm on one provider, and benign routing could not be run live.

\textbf{Conclusion.} We ran five published model-substitution auditors and one new fusion against
a common adversarial gateway, under thresholds sealed before evaluation. Auditing is cheap, at well
under a cent per session, but it is not robust. A gateway that recognises audit-shaped requests
keeps every auditor at chance, and the shape it recognises is the one that makes probes cheap.
Below $\epsilon^* \approx$ 0.10--0.15 no admissible audit detects dilution, because false-positive
budgets, not cost, limit how often an audit can be repeated. Timing auditors flag sanctioned
routing more readily than fraud, which separates identity violations, which content can reveal,
from disclosure and billing violations, which it cannot. Verifiable accounting for LLM APIs will
need probes that cannot be told apart from production traffic, disclosure of routing, and attested
metering. Black-box statistics alone cannot provide the last two.

\textbf{Artifact.} The gateway, auditors, sealed protocols, replay corpus and every figure are
available in an anonymised repository:
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/model-substitution-simu-0F5B}. All results except the live
run can be regenerated without an API key.
