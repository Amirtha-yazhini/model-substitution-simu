\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Auditors, grouped by what they look at.} \emph{Text-based} auditors compare answers with a
reference. Bruckner's one-token test~\cite{bruckner2026} asks short closed questions (``pick a
number from 1 to 100''), caps answers at 16 tokens, and measures how different the two answer
distributions are (Jensen--Shannon divergence). It covered 165 models for \$34, with an equal error
rate of 7.3\%, or 10.6\% with 8 of its 40 probe cells, the version we use. KBF~\cite{fang2026kbf} asks for
numeric facts that sit at the edge of what a model knows, where large and small models of the same
family disagree; on 16 endpoints it flags all 155 substitutions it tested without rejecting any
honest control, and catches dilution of 5--10\% when the two models differ clearly.
IRIS~\cite{iris2026} asks for random numbers or strings, trains a random forest on 179 surface
features of the answers, and sizes its own query budget; it also estimates the dilution rate. \emph{Metadata-based} auditors look at how the service
behaves. GateScope~\cite{lin2026gatescope} audited ten commercial gateways using text, 25-turn memory,
billing mismatches and variation in response time. \emph{Benchmark-based}
auditors~\cite{cai2025} compare accuracy on standard tests (MMLU, GPQA, GSM8K); the same paper
concludes that software-only auditing is unreliable and recommends secure hardware.
\emph{Probability-based} auditors need deeper access: RUT~\cite{zhu2025rut} checks the emitted tokens
against a reference model that the auditor runs locally, and DiFR~\cite{difr2025} needs the
provider to share its random seed so that outputs can be checked against a trusted reference.

\textbf{Replication.} A study that fixed its thresholds before testing~\cite{holdout2026} matched
model families by their reported prompt token counts. It was perfect on development data, but on
fresh data it recognised only half of the same-family pairs, and 6 of 12 fresh pairs could not be
scored because of rate limits and missing usage data. We follow its practice of fixing thresholds in advance, and apply it
to six methods instead of one.

\textbf{What is missing.} Most auditors were tested only against a gateway that cheats the same way
no matter who is asking, and no two were run on the same data. Two papers discuss evasion. RUT is
designed to avoid recognisable query patterns, but has not been tested against an adversary that
knows the method~\cite{zhu2025rut}. IRIS, the closest to our work, tests gateways that spot its
own probes by keyword or by answer shape, and names a fully adaptive gateway as the key open
threat~\cite{iris2026}. We add three things: one adaptive cheater run against
every auditor, one shared set of recorded responses that every auditor sees byte for byte, and
honest load balancing within one session scored as a false-alarm test (KBF's same-model controls
test honest deployments one at a time~\cite{fang2026kbf}). Commercial routers already spread traffic across providers by
default~\cite{openrouter_exacto}, so an auditor that flags mixed traffic will flag them too.
