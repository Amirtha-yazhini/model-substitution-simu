\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
All grid, false-positive and power numbers use protocol v2 and are given as hand-written / fitted
ladder unless stated otherwise.

\subsection{Coverage results}
We probed 14 free-tier model endpoints on three providers, of which 11 returned completions. For
each we recorded whether it returns \texttt{usage}, \texttt{usage.cached\_tokens}, logprobs and
\texttt{system\_fingerprint}, and whether it spends hidden reasoning tokens (Fig.~\ref{fig:F3}).
GATEOPS billing, RUT, OTE with 16 token cap have very low coverage with RUT being zero.
Our RUT version needs endpoint logprobs, which no endpoint returns; the published method instead
needs a locally hosted copy of the model~\cite{zhu2025rut}. The models undergo reasoning steps which requires more tokens
hence, the one token method was changed to have a 256 token cap and the coverage increased
substantially but this could lead to an increase in cost of the probes.

\subsection{The sealed grid}
Each of the twelve arms was audited in 30 sessions per ladder and scored against 500 honest holdout
sessions under the v2 thresholds (Fig.~\ref{fig:F4}). The applicable auditors perform well against
A1, A7, A8, A9 having an AUROC of 1. For A10 the benchmarked based detectors fail as expected, other
detectors succeed as they tend to generalise the probes. OTE and Iris-lite flag genuine altered
sample settings (A4). Both of these use text-only audit and even with correct weights they report a
substitution, because a shifted answer distribution looks like a different model. A5 has the most interesting results an aware adversary can bypass all
the methods. GATEOPS scores benign routing (A11) at AUROC 1.00, which Section~\ref{sec:economics}
examines.

\subsection{False-positive rates v1 and v2}
Under v1 (100 calibration sessions, \texttt{>=} rule) GATEOPS, KBF and BENCH exceeded their required
false positive rate in the 50-session holdout. On 700 further honest sessions, GATEOPS exceeded its
v1 threshold on about 5\% of sessions in every block, so the 0/100 in the calibration block was an
unlucky draw, roughly one in 300. For KBF and BENCH the \texttt{>=} ambiguity increased their false
positive rate. In the v2 run the GATEOPS false positive rate falls to 0\% and it
falls to 0.8\% for KBF and 0.4\% for BENCH over 500 sessions (Fig.~\ref{fig:F5}). This is why v2 was introduced and used along
with a larger, sealed calibration set.

\subsection{Real recorded responses}\label{sec:real}
The census recorded 742 real responses, including one full 240-request OTE session each for
\texttt{gpt-oss-120b} and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, two sizes of one model family. Run directly on these
recordings, OTE is clean on the null (first half of the 120b session against the second half,
Fisher $p = 1.0$). It separates 20b from 120b at $p = 4.5 \times 10^{-6}$, and a different family
(Qwen) from 120b at $p = 3.8 \times 10^{-8}$. The two sizes have different ``random'' preferences:
asked for a number from 1 to 100, the 120b most often answers 73 and the 20b answers 42. Two other
findings point the opposite way. IRIS-lite's surface features cannot separate 20b from 120b on real
text (cross-validated accuracy 0.46, $p = 0.96$), although they do separate a different family
($p = 2.1 \times 10^{-7}$). And Groq returned 50 distinct \texttt{system\_fingerprint} values in 239
honest requests to the 120b, so a changed fingerprint is routine and not evidence of substitution.

\subsection{Live run}
Latency cannot be replayed, so five SHIM servers were placed in front of Groq. An honest reference (REF), A0, A1,
A3 at $\epsilon = 0.10$ and A9 (Table~\ref{tab:live}) were used. Each server received 96 requests
(8 OTE cells $\times$ 12 repeats), timed at the client and interleaved in a new random order each
round so that load drift could not show up as a difference between arms. All 480 requests
succeeded, in 25 minutes and at no cost.

The honest A0 passes both calibration-free tests. The real speed gap is 18\%, far smaller than the
threefold gap the hand-written mock assumed, yet latency still catches full substitution (KS 0.50).
OTE misses it at 12 repeats per cell ($p = 0.10$), although 30 repeats caught the same swap in the
census, so the number of repeats per cell matters. A3, with 14 of 96 requests swapped, passes both
tests. A9 pads responses toward a 0.9\,s mean we chose, with the latency CV GateScope reports
(0.63~\cite{lin2026gatescope}), but Groq answers in 0.66\,s, so the padding made the substitute
slower and noisier than the real model and it was caught (KS 0.43).
